02The sponsor on both sides: what governs now

What was vacated

The SEC’s 2023 Private Fund Adviser Rules, including the mandatory fairness or valuation opinion for adviser-led secondaries, the quarterly statement rule and the audit rule.

Nat’l Ass’n of Private Fund Managers v. SEC, 103 F.4th 1097 (5th Cir. June 5, 2024): vacated in full.

What still governs

  • Advisers Act §206: a non-waivable fiduciary duty of care and loyalty; full and fair disclosure; informed consent.
  • Rule 206(4)-8: antifraud protection for investors in pooled vehicles.
  • The fund agreement: advisory committee consent mechanics and conflict provisions.
  • Delaware law: the agreement can shape fiduciary duties, but the implied covenant survives (6 Del. C. §17-1101(d)).

Losing the rule did not lose the duty. Process now does the work a regulation would have done, and counsel designs the process.

03ILPA’s final continuation vehicle guidance

  • Published October 20, 2026 as Continuation Vehicles: Advancing Alignment Through Best Practices; supersedes the 2023 guidance. Builds on the January 27, 2026 disclosure template and the June 24, 2026 draft.
  • Principles-based: described as designed to guide, not dictate. Not law, but the yardstick committees, arbitrators and examiners will use.
  • Election period: generally no fewer than 20 clear business days after the final election package, longer for complex or multi-asset deals.
  • Economic test: rolling investors, on the whole, no worse off than if the continuation vehicle had not occurred.
  • Departures: explain each one in writing, before the deal.

04Where it goes wrong: process, not paperwork

Abu Dhabi Inv. Council v. Energy & Minerals Grp., C.A. No. 2025-1389-NAC (Del. Ch. filed Dec. 3, 2025). By published accounts the sponsor ran a competitive process, sought committee approval, obtained a fairness opinion and offered cash and roll options. The complaint alleges thin notice and information before the committee vote, unanswered requests for delay and a session without the sponsor, different information for the committee than for buyers, and no process for an IPO alternative. Allegations only; the transaction was paused pending arbitration.

05A defensible continuation vehicle process

1Evidenced rationale

Why a continuation vehicle rather than a sale, IPO, strip sale or distribution in kind, with alternatives actually tested.

2Real price discovery

A process a stranger would call competitive, plus a fairness opinion and an anonymized summary of final bids.

3An informed committee

Early engagement, independent advisers paid by the fund, and a session without the sponsor.

4Time and information parity

One data room and one narrative for investors and buyers; the election clock runs from a complete package.

5Real choices

Sell, roll, roll with top-up or split. A status-quo option on existing terms is the benchmark; explain if not offered.

6Sponsor alignment

Full carry rollover absent a stated reason; disclose any de-risking; costs follow the parties who benefit.

07Company tender offers

  1. Is it a tender offer? No statutory definition. Courts weigh the Wellman v. Dickinson factors or the Hanson Trust totality approach. A company-wide employee program almost always qualifies.
  2. Regulation 14E applies even to non-reporting companies: minimum offering period, Rule 14e-3 trading limits, and Section 14(e) antifraud. Rule 13e-4 applies only to reporting issuers.
  3. The April 16, 2026 exemptive order permits a 10-business-day minimum period for a non-reporting company’s own self-tender (by the company or a wholly owned subsidiary). Offers by outside buyers still run 20 business days. Decide the structure first and the calendar second.

When the company buys from its own employees, the offer document is the whole of their protection: how the price was set, recent 409A value and financing terms, preferences, material developments, risks and tax consequences.
